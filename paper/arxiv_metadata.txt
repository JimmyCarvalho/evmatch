Title: Evidence-conditioned correlated matching for surface codes under circuit-level noise

Abstract:
Matching decoders turn each error mechanism of a circuit-level detector error model into graph edges and give every edge the mechanism's full probability, even when the syndrome rules the mechanism out. We show that a local, syndrome-dependent correction to the edge weights brings correlated minimum-weight perfect matching to, or beyond, the accuracy of belief-matching. The rule is that a multi-edge mechanism contributes probability to its edges only if each of its edges touches a detection event. It costs O(#detection events) per shot, and we combine it with the standard two-pass correlated reweighting. For the rotated surface-code memory under SI1000 noise at distances 5, 7 and 9 and physical error rates 0.2-0.5%, the resulting decoder makes 7-17% fewer logical errors than the correlated mode of PyMatching 2. The advantage grows with distance. In paired tests it is statistically indistinguishable from belief-matching at d=5,7 and better at d=9, while running at 4.6-7.2x the time of uncorrelated PyMatching (1.7-2.2x its correlated mode). The decoder is open source, sinter-compatible and built on PyMatching's sparse blossom.
